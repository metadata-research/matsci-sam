# Communities and scope

A community is a group of people, such as a lab or review panel, working
together on terminology and, when enabled, metadata fields. Each community has
its own vocabulary, members and surveys. Its worklist contains collections of
terms selected for review.

| Object     | Purpose                                                                           |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Community  | Brings people together to contribute and review.                                  |
| Vocabulary | Names a body of terms and their definitions. Each term belongs to one vocabulary. |
| Collection | Selects existing terms for a purpose, including terms from several vocabularies.  |

Use **Import terms** to prepare a batch of new terms as private drafts. Use a
collection to gather existing terms without copying their definitions.

## Community workspace

The community selector beside **MatSci-SAM** shows your selected community.
Choose one of your communities to open it and use its vocabulary for new terms
started from Home. Selecting a membership from **My communities** or the
community directory does the same. Pending draft changes save before switching.
**All communities** includes every vocabulary you can read. **Find communities**
opens the directory. Visitors and accounts without memberships also have a
**Communities** link in the header.

Browsing a community you have not joined does not select it or grant access.
Copied links and links opened in another tab remain ordinary browsing links.
Actions on a community page use its own vocabulary. Saved drafts keep their
destination, and the editor shows the actual **Vocabulary** throughout entry.
The community description appears beneath the title when you can read its
content.

The community page opens on **Vocabulary**, with all its terms or reference
fields available. Choose a term to read its definitions or propose a change. A
conference invitation opens its selected review after you sign in and join.

| Tab            | Purpose                                                                                       |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Vocabulary** | Find published terms or metadata fields and add a term.                                       |
| **Reviews**    | Respond to feedback requests, inspect shared definitions and proposals, or organize a review. |
| **My work**    | Resume your private drafts and find your submitted or published contributions.                |
| **More**       | Open People, Collections, Getting started, About and available Surveys.                       |

**My work** appears when you are signed in. Authorized stewards and site
administrators find **Manage community** under **More**. Review creation and
packet export remain in **Reviews**.

**More → About** gives the community description and content access rules.
**Getting started** provides links to respond to a review, propose a change
or resume your work. Private drafts are in **My work**.

Ordinary vocabularies open as an alphabetical list. A metadata community uses
**Hierarchy**, with **Terms** available alongside **Metadata fields**. When a
reference catalog is installed, it opens the latest **Reference standard** with
sections collapsed. Expand a section, search for a field, or select a profile.
**Community version** shows accepted community changes. **Community releases**
lists named snapshots. Pending proposals appear under **Reviews → Definitions
and proposals** and do not change the community version.

In longer views, **On this page** lists the available sections. Select one to
jump to it. The destination is briefly highlighted. On a small screen, open the
compact **On this page** menu. Short views omit this menu.

Draft and import editors opened from **My work** provide a link back to the
community, retaining your section, field filter and result page. If a draft has
unsaved changes, the return waits for saving. A save failure leaves you in the
editor with your writing available.

Use **Add a term** for a vocabulary entry or **Import terms** to prepare a batch
of private drafts. These actions use the community vocabulary. Your drafts
remain private even when other contributors belong to the same community.

In **More → Manage community**, stewards and site administrators can enable
metadata development to add **New metadata field**. MatCore is optional. An
independent field can record value types, units and requirement rules with its
own citations. The [metadata workflow guide](/docs/matcore-workflow) explains
field editing and MatCore reference fields and proposals.

In **Vocabulary → Metadata fields**, choose **Reference standard** and a
reference version. **Find a field** searches entries in that version and profile
by name, path or definition. Open a field and select **Propose a change** to
create a private draft. **Continue my draft** resumes your existing work,
including drafts imported earlier. Browsing the catalog creates no drafts. The
reference version remains unchanged when you edit or submit a proposal. **View
proposal** opens a submitted proposal, where **Propose a revision** starts
further changes.

Source versions and local proposal revisions are separate. Existing drafts and
proposals keep their source version when another snapshot becomes available.
They are not updated automatically. Stewards can accept changes into the
community version and create named community releases. Each release preserves
its field contents and review decisions. Community review and releases do not
establish MatCore approval.

## Review and respond

**My work → Requested feedback** lists open reviews for your selected area. Your
progress is personal. **Start review** opens the first unanswered item, and
**Continue review** resumes the next one. Participation is optional. A shared
request does not create a private draft or require you to respond.

Open a review and read the entry, version and field rules. Choose **Suitable as
written**, **Needs revision**, or **Unable to assess**, add an optional comment,
and select **Save and next**. You can also save a comment without choosing a
response. **Clear choice** leaves your comment in place. For a proposed change,
the first response is **Support this change**. Some rounds use upvotes and
downvotes.

The four main field rules stay visible. Expand **Value guidance and field
rules** to read the source text and remaining details. **Not recorded
separately** means a structured rule is missing while value guidance is
available. Open **Comment (optional)** to add a comment. Saved and recovered
comments open when you return to the item.

**Not yet reviewed**, **My responses** and **All items** let you choose what to
see. **Skip for now** leaves an item unanswered. You can change or withdraw a
saved response. Organizers can see who responded, their comments and the
versions they reviewed. Other participants see totals and their own responses.
Votes inform review. A steward decision is required to accept a proposal into
the community version.

**Suggest a change** opens a private field draft. **Back to review** saves the
draft and returns to the same review item. If a reference field already has a
proposal, the draft starts from that proposal or resumes your existing private
alternative. The reference stays unchanged. For an ordinary definition, use
**Propose a change**. A notice identifies a newer revision if the review used an
earlier one. **Back to review** returns to the original review item.

Save failures leave your response available for retry. When browser storage is
available, this tab also keeps unsaved responses across reloads. If another tab
has saved a newer response, **Reload saved response** replaces the text in this
form with that saved version. Copy any text you want to keep first.

### Use a dataset example

Some reviews include an optional **Dataset example**. Name a real or published
dataset and enter its URL, citation or description. Download the template for
the version shown, complete its XML and paste it into **Completed metadata
XML**. Add any notes and check your name and affiliation, then save. You can
also choose **Review fields** without adding an example.

The XML check verifies syntax. It does not validate field values or certify that
a dataset meets the standard. `FILL` placeholders indicate unfinished values.
Each template covers its selected section. Add inherited sections as directed by
the standard.

Your saved example is visible to you and community stewards. Feedback records
the exact example revision used when you save it. Later example edits do not
rewrite earlier responses. If another tab changes the example, reload the saved
example and review it before saving your feedback again.

Unsaved writing stays in this browser tab when session storage is available. An
interrupted save can be retried. A conflict preserves your writing until you
choose **Reload saved dataset example**. Copy text you want to keep first.

### Organize a review

Stewards and site administrators can open **Reviews → Request feedback**. Name
the round, choose an area and response style, and select the reference fields,
pending proposals or ordinary definitions to include. Open **Choose items**, use
**Clear selection**, then **Find a term, field or proposal** to locate the items
you want. Select the reference, proposal, or both. Selection is retained when
you search for another item.

For a metadata review, **Include a dataset exercise** adds the optional example
form. Select a reference version and area, then add brief guidance or a review
guide link. Existing reviews keep their original setup.

**Create review** records the exact versions and labels and adds the review to
**Requested feedback** in My work for community members. Members can respond
whenever they are ready. No notification is sent. Create a new round to review
later versions or a different selection.

Choose **View results** on a review to see participant responses. Its management
page also lets you create or revoke conference invitations and close the round.
Closing stops new responses and admissions. Previous responses remain available.

A conference invitation lets anyone holding its link create an account and
explicitly join the community, including its private sections. It opens the
selected review after joining. Choose an expiry of 1 to 30 days and revoke the
link when registration ends. Signing in alone does not grant community access.

## Content access

A community can share its content publicly or limit it to members. Its name
appears in the community directory. For **Members only** communities, visitors
see the name and an option to request access. Vocabulary entries, sources,
proposals and activity remain restricted. Site administrators can also read
shared content. Personal drafts remain visible only to their authors.

Sign in and select **Request access** on the community page. The page shows when
your request is awaiting review. A steward or site administrator can open **More
→ People**, find **Access requests**, and approve or decline it. Check the same
community page for the outcome. This does not send an email notification.

Stewards can open **More → Manage community** and use **Content access** to
choose **Public** or **Members only**, then select **Save content access**. A
community containing protected source material must remain members-only. An
installed MatCore source requires a separate grant from a site administrator.
Enabling MatCore tools does not make sources from another community available.

Removing a member ends their access to restricted content and saved drafts in
that community. Their records are preserved. Restoring membership lets them
resume their own drafts. A restriction cannot recall files already downloaded or
text already copied by an authorized reader.

## Home and guided setup

Home has term search, **Browse terms**, **Add a term**, **Create account** and
**Join a community**. After sign-in, **My communities** links directly to each
community page. Use the community selector to switch between memberships or open
**Find communities**. **Browse terms** follows that selection. **All
communities** includes the vocabularies you can read.

Use **Add a term** on Home for the selected community, or the general MatSci-SAM
vocabulary when **All communities** is selected. The same action in a community
**Vocabulary** view uses that community. **My work** provides **Add a term** and
**Import terms** for the displayed community. Check the vocabulary named in the
editor before submitting.

Open **More → Collections** to manage community worklists and create a
collection when your role allows it. This includes collections used in surveys.
Each community has one vocabulary. **More →
Manage community** links to its vocabulary details. Site administrators create
communities under **Administration → Communities**. Creating a community creates
its vocabulary as well. Vocabulary creation remains an administrator task.

[Guided setup](/setup) remains available for related tasks. Reuse an existing
community or vocabulary and skip the optional collection when it is not needed.
Creation saves a vocabulary or collection immediately. Term drafts remain
private until you review and publish them. Setup choices and pasted import text
remain in this browser on this device when storage is available.

For an import, upload a CSV or TSV file or paste its contents. Select new rows
to save as private drafts, then return through **My work** to review and
publish. Existing definitions remain unchanged. The
[import guide](/docs/importing-terms) lists the supported columns, source
options and limits.

## Working in a community

Community links open that community directly. They do not move an existing draft
or change its reference version. The global **My work** link opens term drafts
and imports across communities, with a link to published contributions. Metadata
field drafts and submitted proposals are in **My work** within the relevant
community.

Navigation from an edited term or metadata draft waits for saving. If saving
fails, the editor stays open so you can retry. Existing review, version and
section links retain their destination.

A collection created within a members-only community follows its content access.
Linking an existing public collection into a private workspace does not make the
collection private. Readers see only terms they are authorized to read.

A collection can reference terms from other vocabularies. Those terms retain
their original vocabulary and identifiers. Two communities can define the same
label independently, with separate definitions, votes and rankings.
Contributions to existing terms remain in their original vocabulary.

## Studies

Community members review selected terms in a study, following instructions
within an optional participation window. Begin from the study page or **Your
surveys** on your profile. An invitation can add membership before opening the
activity. Members-only surveys use terms from their own community vocabulary.
Public surveys can continue to use terms from other vocabularies that
participants can read. [Studies](/docs/studies) explains Position choices and
saved progress.

## Joining

Open a personal invitation or reusable group link. Select **Create an account**
if you are new, or **Sign in** if you have an account. Choose an available
Google, ORCID or email method. After creating an account, add your name or
select **Skip for now**. You return to the invitation to select **Join** with
the community name. Signing in or creating an account does not accept the
invitation.

A reusable join link created by a steward lets recipients explicitly join as
ordinary members. For a members-only community, joining grants access to its
private content. A community directory link instead offers **Request access**
when you do not have an invitation.

A personal invitation works with an existing account even if it was sent to
another email address. It can be used once and expires after fourteen days.
Request a replacement if it expires or is withdrawn or replaced. Reusable group
links remain active until disabled or replaced.

See [Account access](/docs/account-access) for sign-in methods. You can leave a
community from its page.

## Membership visibility

Members and site administrators can view the roster. Other visitors see the
member count for public communities. Members-only communities show neither the
roster nor its count to visitors. Your
[profile setting](/docs/account-access#profile-visibility) controls public
profile access and affiliation display. Rosters and invitations are omitted from
public metadata exports.

## Stewardship and administration

Stewards manage content access, membership requests, members, worklists,
invitations, and surveys in assigned communities. Site administrators can
create, rename, retire, and restore communities and appoint stewards. Renaming
preserves the address. Retirement retains membership history and the worklist
for restoration.

[Administration and stewardship](/docs/administration) lists the controls by
role.

## Select contributions for review

In **Reviews → Definitions and proposals**, filter by type or status and select
the revisions to review. Ordinary communities label this list **Definitions**. A
steward can choose **Request feedback**, name the review and check its items
before creating it. Term definitions and metadata proposals can belong to the
same round. Reviews for a selected area include term definitions from across the
community only when the steward selects that option.

Responses stay attached to the versions shown in that round. Published term
definitions remain available for voting. Metadata decisions separately control
which proposals enter the community version.

## Export a review packet

A steward can select contributions and choose **Export review packet**, or open
**View results** for a review to export its selected revisions. The preview
lists exact revisions, source counts and review responses. Respondent names are
omitted unless **Include respondent names** is selected. Author attribution
remains in the report. **Include dataset examples** adds the saved example
revisions attached to the selected feedback. This is a separate choice from
including names. Dataset text may identify its author.

Choose **Print / save PDF** for a printable report or **Download JSON** for the
same structured information. The report includes changes, sources, discussion,
feedback by review round and recorded decisions. A pending proposal can be
included without accepting it. Exporting changes no community records.

If content, access or feedback changes after preview, refresh the preview before
downloading. Private drafts, inaccessible source material and private survey
participation are excluded. Downloaded copies retain their contents after access
changes, so share a packet only with its intended readers.
