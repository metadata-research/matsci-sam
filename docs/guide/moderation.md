# Moderation and publication limits

Administrators can hide a term from listings and restore it later. Contributors
also have limits on how many definitions, comments and examples they can publish
within a short period. This guide explains these controls and what to do when
you encounter them.

## Hide a term from listings

Sign in as a site administrator and open **Visibility** in Administration.
The moderator role and community stewardship alone do not grant this control.
See [Administration and stewardship](/docs/administration#roles-and-scope)
for the available roles.

1. Use **Find a term** to search by name. Check the vocabulary and the term
   itself before making a decision. Narrow the search if it returns too many
   matches.
2. Choose **Hide** beside a listed term.
3. Enter a **Public reason for hiding**. This reason will appear at the existing
   term address. Keep personal information and other private details out of it.
4. Select **Hide from listings** to confirm. **Cancel** leaves the term unchanged.

Review the actual content before hiding it. A term name or contributor name alone
does not establish that it is test content. The control applies to the whole
term, including discovery of its competing definitions.

## What a hidden term retains

A hidden term leaves search, Browse, recent Discussion, term suggestions and
current vocabulary downloads. It is excluded from new study assignments
generated from collections. Existing study steps and recorded responses remain.

Existing term, definition, revision and provenance links still open the published
content with a hidden-term notice and the public reason. Those pages ask search
engines not to index them. Hiding does not make content private or remove copies
already downloaded. Direct term downloads and historical provenance retain the
content with a visibility marker.

Hiding preserves identifiers, definitions, comments, votes, examples and revision
history. Existing contribution permissions still apply at direct links. It does
not lock discussion. The term also retains its name within its vocabulary, so
submitting the same label cannot create a second listed copy.

## Restore a term

Return to **Visibility**, find the hidden term and choose **Restore**. Enter a
**Reason for restoration**, then select **Restore to listings**. The restoration
reason is saved in administrator history. It is not a public hiding notice.

Expand **Visibility history** in the dialog to review recorded decisions, with
their administrator, time and reason. Restoration returns the existing term to
listings and keeps its contribution history. If another administrator changes
visibility while you are reviewing it, refresh and review the current state
before trying again.

## Publication limits

The following limits apply per account, including administrators and study
participants. Definitions, comments and examples have separate limits.

| New publication | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Definition | 5 in 10 minutes |
| Comment | 10 in 1 minute |
| Example added to an existing definition | 10 in 1 minute |

When a limit message appears, wait until earlier publications fall outside that
time window before trying again. Editing an existing definition or restoring a
definition revision uses the existing revision checks rather than these limits
on new publications. An example included when publishing a definition is part
of that definition submission. File uploads have separate
[size and pending-file limits](/docs/files-and-examples).

Submitting the same contribution again within 60 seconds may produce
"This contribution was just submitted. Refresh the page to see it before trying
again." Refresh and check whether the contribution is already present before
submitting again. Changing the wording solely to retry can create an unwanted
second contribution.
