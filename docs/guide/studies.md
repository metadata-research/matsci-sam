# Studies

A study is a piece of work a community takes on: a named set of terms,
instructions that say what to do with them, and a window in which to do it.
Each study has a page at `/studies/<slug>` that anyone can read. The page
holds the instructions, the terms, a link to the walkthrough where the study
has one, and the list the group has agreed on so far. [Communities and
scope](/docs/communities) covers the community that runs a study and how you
join it.

## The study page

"What to do" is the text the steward wrote, the same text an invitation
opens on, and "The terms" links to the collection the study works through.
The window of the study says when acts are accepted.

A study with a walkthrough shows a card with your place in it. **Start the
walkthrough** begins it, **Continue** returns you to the step you stopped at,
and the card says so when you have finished. The card appears only while you
belong to the community and the study is open.

## The walkthrough

The walkthrough is the ordered steps the study asks of you. The list of steps
on the page shows where you are, and a step you have completed can be
reopened and read but not changed. The first step is the instructions. Then
come a position step for each term, a review step for each term, and the
closing questions. The button at the end of a step saves it, and you can
leave and come back at any point.

The pilot protocol settles a draft list. Each term has a draft definition,
the one a model generated in the earlier round, with the comments that round
left on it. Some drafts are wrong, in wording or in kind. The work includes
amending or replacing a draft, and a draft nobody corrects stands.

### Taking a position

A position step shows the candidates of one term, the draft first and then,
under "Proposed so far", each definition proposed since, in order of support,
with its votes and comments. You take one position on the term.

**Accept** records an upvote on that candidate as your position. No text is
copied. **Amend** opens the definition form with the text of that candidate
filled in, and what you publish is a definition of your own, recorded as
derived from the candidate you started from. **None of these work** opens an
empty form, and what you publish joins the candidates as your position.
Amend the candidate closest to what you would use, so a later reader has a
text to endorse and the list stays short.

Once you have taken a position the step shows the candidate you accepted or
proposed, and **Continue** moves on. An upvote you cast on a candidate before
the round stands, and the step says so under that candidate. **Accept** then
records it as your position without casting a second vote. If you already
have a definition of the term from outside the walkthrough, the move open to
you is to accept a candidate.

### Reviewing the candidates

A review step compares the candidates of a term where more than one was
proposed when you open the step. Each candidate shows its votes and a comment
box, the order does not change as you vote, and **Done with this term**
completes the step. Where a term has one candidate there is nothing to
compare. The step says that the one candidate stands with the support it has,
and the same button completes it.

A comment you leave inside a review step is read by the group. It does not
prompt the model to revise its draft, which an ordinary comment on the
current text of a model definition does.

### The closing questions

The last steps are questions about the list as a whole. A scale question
takes an answer from 1 to 5, and a text question takes a few sentences.
**Submit** records the answer, once. The default questions ask whether you
would use the list as it stands in your work, and what is missing from it or
wrong in it.

## The agreed list

For each term, the study page shows the definition with the most support as
the agreed definition of the group so far. Support is the upvotes less the
downvotes on the current text of a definition, and a tie goes to the earlier
candidate. Nobody declares a definition agreed. The list moves as positions
are taken.

## When a study closes

A study accepts positions, votes, comments and answers only while it is open
and only from members of its community. Once it has closed, its address
still resolves and its agreed list counts the votes as they stood on the day
it closed. What its participants contributed stays in the vocabulary.

<details>
<summary>Running a study</summary>

A steward starts a study from the community page, as [Communities and
scope](/docs/communities) describes, and each study listed there has a
control for its walkthrough.

**Generate the walkthrough** builds the steps from the collection of the
study. The steps are the instructions, a position and a review step for each
term, and, with the box ticked, the two closing questions. **Regenerate**
replaces them until somebody has started, after which the walkthrough is in
use and its steps are not replaced. The line under the control counts how
many members of the community have finished.

When the window closes, the walkthrough stops accepting acts and the agreed
list becomes the outcome. The terms and their definitions stay open on the
term pages.
