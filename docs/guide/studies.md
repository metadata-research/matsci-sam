# Studies

A study asks a community to work through a selected collection of terms under
shared instructions. Participants choose or propose definitions, review them,
and answer any closing questions.

## Help in the study

Select **Study help** at the top of any activity step. Help opens over the
activity, starting with the topic for that step. Choose **This study’s
instructions** to reread the instructions, or another topic for an explanation
of the actions. **Back to study** returns to the same step with your draft
still on screen. Opening help does not submit an answer or complete a step.

The help topics use the same text as this guide. Guide links open in a new
tab so you can keep the study open. Follow your study’s own instructions for
its purpose and tasks; this guide explains how the controls work.

Use **Feedback** to report a problem with the site. Use the comment box in a
Review step to discuss a definition. These are separate conversations.

## The study page

The public page at `/studies/{study}` describes the study, its community,
terms, and closing date. Anyone can read it. To participate, sign in with the
account that belongs to the community running the study. Existing members
also see the study under **Your studies** on their profile and on the Studies
page. They do not need a new invitation. A new participant joins through an
invitation or is added by a steward; the public study link alone does not add
membership. If you cannot begin, check that you signed in with the account
you used before. Ask the person who shared the study link to add you or send
an invitation if you need to join. See
[Communities and scope](/docs/communities#joining).

**Begin study** opens the activity. **Continue study** returns to your next
unfinished step. **About this study** gives the overview; the first activity
step presents the instructions. **The terms** links to the selected collection.

## Terms used in the study

- **Term:** a concept in one vocabulary. Two communities can use the same
  word for different concepts.
- **Definition:** one contributed interpretation of a term. The same term
  can have several competing definitions, also called **candidates**.
- **Revision:** an exact version of one definition’s wording. Votes apply to
  the revision shown. Editing a definition creates a new revision; suggesting
  an alternative creates a separate definition.
- **Position:** the definition you accept or publish for a term in the study.
  It records your choice at that step. It is separate from your later votes
  and from the community’s canonical definition.
- **Canonical definition:** the current highest-ranked definition for the
  term in its owning vocabulary. Highest net score wins, then newest
  definition creation time, then higher permanent definition number. This
  does not mean that every study participant agrees with it.
- **Support score:** upvotes minus downvotes on the current revision. It can
  include votes from outside this study. It is not a count of study positions.

**Suggest a revision** is the name of an AI-assisted action. Despite the
word “revision,” publishing its draft creates another definition with its
own revision history. The source definition stays available. See
[AI-assisted suggestions](/docs/ai-refinement#suggest-a-revision-to-a-definition).

## The study activity

The current walkthrough uses this sequence:

1. Read the instructions.
2. Take a **Position** on each term.
3. **Review** each term’s definitions.
4. Answer any closing **Questions**.

You work through all Position steps before the Review steps. The number of
terms and closing questions varies by study.

The step indicators show your progress. Completed steps can be reopened for
reading. Your first unfinished step is available; later unfinished steps
remain locked. A dash marks both steps for a term you skipped.

## The Position step

Choose the definition closest to what you consider correct. You may choose
your own definition and any definition regardless of its support score.
Existing definitions may come from earlier vocabulary work, preparation for
this study, or other participants. If none exists, propose the first one or
skip the term.

The current Position view puts the earliest model-authored definition first
when one exists. The others follow by support score, with older definitions
first for equal scores. **First on this screen does not mean canonical or
recommended.** Attribution, comments, and scores provide context; the vote
arrows appear in Review.

### Accept a definition

**Accept this definition** records the exact revision as your position and
ensures that you upvote it. If you already upvoted it, that vote remains and
no extra point is added. If you downvoted it, Accept changes that vote to an
upvote. This differs from selecting the up arrow again, which withdraws an
existing upvote.

Accepting completes your Position step. An upvote cast elsewhere does not
replace this explicit choice inside the study.

### Suggest a revision

Use **Revise this definition** to open **Suggest a revision** when a
definition is close but needs a change.
Explain what is wrong or missing, request a language-model draft, and review
and edit it. **Publish revision** adds it as a separate definition, credits
you and the named model, and records its exact source revision. The original
remains available. Publication records your position and completes the step;
it does not cast a vote. You can vote on it during Review.

Before a draft exists, you can accept the source as written or return to the
definitions. **Discard draft** publishes nothing. If model drafting is
unavailable, you can return and use **Propose a new definition** to write your
own alternative. Comments belong in the Review step.

### Propose a new definition

Use **Propose a new definition** when none of the available definitions is
close enough, or when the term has no definition yet. Write the definition
yourself; an optional example can show how it is used.

**Publish new definition** adds a separate definition of the term, records
your position, and completes the step without casting a vote. It does not
identify another definition as a replacement target. Existing definitions
remain available. This is distinct from **Propose a replacement** on an
ordinary definition page.

### Skip a term

Use **Skip this term** when you do not know the term well enough to choose.
Confirming skips both its Position and Review steps. It records no position,
vote, definition, or comment, and does not change the vocabulary. Skipping is
final within this walkthrough. A later skipped Review can be opened for
reading, but does not unlock other unfinished steps.

## Reviewing the definitions

Review the definitions and use the upvote, downvote, and comment controls.
You may vote on your own definition. These controls are also available when
there is only one definition. If there are no definitions, continue to the
next step.

Each account has one vote per current definition revision. Select an up or
down arrow to support or oppose it. Select the same arrow again to withdraw
that vote; select the other arrow to change it. **If Accept already upvoted
your choice, pressing up again removes that upvote.** Publishing your own
proposal in Position did not vote for it automatically.

A Review step starts in the normal canonical order. Cards stay in place as
you vote, so the leading card can stop being the highest-scoring definition.
Reviewing does not replace the Position you recorded earlier.

Comments are public discussion on a definition. A comment records exactly
what you write and never prompts a language model. Votes and comments made
here are vocabulary contributions with this study step recorded as their
context. The displayed score and comment history can also include activity
from outside the study. See [Voting and score](/docs/community#voting-and-score).

Select **Done with this term** when you have finished reviewing it. Votes and
comments save when you submit each action; **Done** records that the step is
complete. The interface does not require a vote on every definition to enable
Done, so follow the study instructions. After completion, the step is read-only.

## The closing questions

Answer the question shown. A scale question accepts one value from 1 to 5;
read its endpoint labels. A text question accepts a written response.
**Submit** records the answer and completes the step. A saved answer can be
reread but not edited in the walkthrough.

The default questions ask whether you would use the list in your work and
what you would add or change. A study may use different questions or none.
Use **Feedback** for a site problem rather than treating a closing question
as a support request.

## Saving and returning

Recorded positions, skips, published definitions, votes, comments, and
submitted answers are saved. Return through the study link or **Your studies**
on your profile, then select **Continue study**. It resumes at the first
unfinished step.

Text still being written in a form is a draft, not a saved contribution.
Opening and closing **Study help** keeps it on screen, but leaving or
reloading the activity can lose it. A saved vote or comment does not by itself
complete a Review step; select **Done with this term** when finished.

Completed steps can be reopened for reading. **Review completed study** opens
your consolidated record: the position or skip for each term, votes and
comments recorded inside Review steps, and closing answers. Later vocabulary
activity can change scores or wording without rewriting your recorded choice.

At closing time, the study stops accepting actions. Its page and recorded
contributions remain available. Definitions, votes, and comments also remain
in the vocabulary and its [provenance](/docs/provenance).

## Study and vocabulary workflows

The study uses the same terms, definition identities, revisions, votes, and
comments as the rest of MatSci-SAM. It adds an ordered task and a record of
your participation. Working on an ordinary vocabulary page does not complete
a study step; use the activity to record the study’s requested actions.

| Action or concept        | Ordinary vocabulary work                               | Inside the current study walkthrough                                                                                   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Choose a definition      | Read, compare, and vote freely                         | Accept or publish one Position per term, or skip it                                                                    |
| Suggest a revision       | Publish a separate AI-assisted definition              | Same publication, also recorded as your Position                                                                       |
| Write an alternative     | Propose a replacement for a particular definition      | Propose a new definition for the term without a replacement target                                                     |
| Edit your own definition | Publish another revision under its existing identifier | The Position choices publish a separate alternative; they do not edit an existing definition in place                  |
| Vote and comment         | Available on current definitions                       | Accept ensures an upvote; other voting and commenting happen in Review                                                 |
| Definition order         | Canonical first; newest wins a score tie               | Position puts a model-authored starting definition first when available; Review keeps its initial order while you vote |
| Progress                 | No required sequence                                   | Recorded steps, saved progress, and a closing window                                                                   |

A definition’s permanent number is its identity within the term, not its
screen position or score. To cite exact wording, use the immutable revision
link; see [Identifiers and citation](/docs/identifiers#citation).

## When a study closes

The closing date on the study page determines when participation ends. After
closing, new positions, skips, votes, comments, and answers cannot be added
through the activity. Vocabulary work outside the study can continue and is
not retroactively counted as study participation.

## Study administration

A steward or administrator prepares the collection, instructions, steps,
questions, and participation window. Selected exclusions can limit the
definitions offered in that study without deleting them from the vocabulary.

Prepare and review the starting material and instructions before recruiting.
The study instructions should explain its purpose, starting material, and
tasks. Stored instructions remain the record of what participants were asked
to do.

[Administration and stewardship](/docs/administration#studies) describes the
controls for setup, invitations, and progress.
